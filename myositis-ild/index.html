DERMATOMYOSITIS-SPECTRUM MSA

RP-ILD · CADM

Anti-MDA5

Signature: clinically amyopathic/hypomyopathic DM, ulcerative/palmar skin disease, arthritis; strongest MSA association with acute/subacute or rapidly progressive ILD.

Think next: Ro52, ferritin/LDH, oxygenation, HRCT burden, infection risk under intensive immunosuppression.

CANCER SIGNAL

Anti-TIF1γ

Signature: adult dermatomyositis with strong malignancy association and characteristic cutaneous phenotype.

ILD: comparatively uncommon; do not infer an MDA5-like lung phenotype from TIF1γ positivity.

DM · MALIGNANCY / CALCINOSIS CONTEXT

Anti-NXP2

Signature: severe muscle phenotype, dysphagia/edema; calcinosis is particularly relevant in juvenile disease. Adult cancer association varies across cohorts.

ILD: not a classic dominant pulmonary serotype.

CLASSIC DM

Anti-Mi-2α / Mi-2β

Signature: classic cutaneous DM with prominent muscle involvement and generally favorable treatment response.

ILD: usually less prominent than in MDA5 or antisynthetase phenotypes.

DM · DYSPHAGIA

Anti-SAE1

Signature: skin disease may precede muscle disease; dysphagia can be prominent.

ILD: reported, but pulmonary risk is less consistently defined than MDA5/ARS; commercial assay interpretation deserves care.

IMMUNE-MEDIATED NECROTIZING MYOPATHY

IMNM · SEVERE MYOPATHY

Anti-SRP

Signature: severe proximal weakness, marked CK elevation and necrotizing pathology.

ILD: can occur, but an independent MDA5-like RP-ILD phenotype is not established. Weak line-blot positivity without a matching phenotype deserves confirmation.

IMNM · STATIN CONTEXT

Anti-HMGCR

Signature: necrotizing autoimmune myopathy, often but not exclusively associated with statin exposure; persistent weakness/CK despite statin withdrawal is a clue.

ILD: not a defining feature; think muscle-first.

IBM / SUPPORTIVE SEROLOGY

SUPPORTIVE · NOT SPECIFIC

Anti-cN1A / NT5C1A

Signature: supportive marker for inclusion-body myositis when the clinical pattern fits.

Caveat: not sufficiently specific to diagnose IBM alone; positivity also occurs in other autoimmune diseases.

MYOSITIS-ASSOCIATED / OVERLAP AUTOANTIBODIES

SEVERITY MODIFIER

Anti-Ro52 / TRIM21

Why interesting: a modifier rather than a stand-alone myositis subtype. Particularly informative with MDA5; in ASyS it tracks pulmonary burden/relapse more consistently than universal mortality.

SCLEROMYOSITIS OVERLAP

Anti-PM/Scl-75 · PM/Scl-100

Signature: myositis–systemic-sclerosis overlap, Raynaud, mechanic’s hands, ILD and sometimes calcinosis.

Clinical pearl: the overlap phenotype can be more informative than forcing classification into “pure” myositis or SSc.

OVERLAP · ASSAY CAUTION

Anti-Ku

Signature: overlap CTD with myositis and possible ILD; phenotype varies substantially by cohort.

Caveat: interpret commercial assay positivity in clinical context.

MCTD / OVERLAP

Anti-U1-RNP

Signature: mixed connective-tissue/overlap phenotype with Raynaud, arthritis, myositis and pulmonary involvement.

Think next: ILD and pulmonary hypertension are different pulmonary problems and should not be conflated.

Assay reality: commercial line/dot blots are useful screening tools, but false positives and false negatives remain important. A 2025 systematic review found pooled specificity generally high but highlighted substantial assay-dependent sensitivity and false-positive concerns, particularly for line-blot testing. Phenotype–serology discordance matters.

THE ANTISYNTHETASE SPECTRUM

Same syndrome family.
Different pulmonary personalities.Ίδια οικογένεια συνδρόμου.
Διαφορετικές πνευμονικές προσωπικότητες.

The core clinically established panel includes eight anti-aminoacyl-tRNA synthetase antibodies. Rare additional synthetase targets are being described, but their disease specificity and routine diagnostic value are less mature.Το βασικό κλινικά καθιερωμένο panel περιλαμβάνει οκτώ anti-aminoacyl-tRNA synthetase αντισώματα. Περιγράφονται και σπανιότερα synthetase targets, αλλά η ειδικότητα νόσου και η routine diagnostic αξία τους είναι λιγότερο ώριμες.

HISTIDYL-tRNA SYNTHETASE

Jo-1 · HisRS

Most common ARS. Classic combination of myositis, ILD, inflammatory arthritis, mechanic’s hands, Raynaud and fever; disease features may accumulate over time rather than appear simultaneously.

THREONYL-tRNA SYNTHETASE

PL-7 · ThrRS

Often strongly lung-relevant; non-Jo-1 disease may present as ILD before overt myositis. Keep the individual antibody rather than coding only “ASyS positive”.

ALANYL-tRNA SYNTHETASE

PL-12 · AlaRS

Frequently lung-dominant or amyopathic/hypomyopathic compared with classic Jo-1 phenotypes; isolated ILD should not stop you from considering ASyS.

GLYCYL-tRNA SYNTHETASE

EJ · GlyRS

Rare but highly relevant in ILD practice. Acute/subacute interstitial pneumonia may precede obvious muscle disease; relapse biology deserves attention.

ISOLEUCYL-tRNA SYNTHETASE

OJ · IleRS

Rare and diagnostically challenging. OJ antigenicity involves a multisynthetase complex, and some routine assays may under-detect it; phenotype can be strongly pulmonary.

ASPARAGINYL-tRNA SYNTHETASE

KS · AsnRS

Very rare; often discussed in lung-dominant presentations. The absence of florid myositis does not exclude an antisynthetase phenotype.

PHENYLALANYL-tRNA SYNTHETASE

Zo · PheRS

Rare ARS specificity. Useful mostly in expert serology/phenotype workups when common ARS antibodies are negative but the clinical picture remains convincing.

TYROSYL-tRNA SYNTHETASE

Ha / YRS · TyrRS

Rare established ARS specificity. Data are sparse, so avoid pretending its prognosis is as well quantified as Jo-1, PL-7 or PL-12.

RESEARCH FRONTIER

Other rare ARS targets

Reports include additional synthetase targets such as valyl/cysteinyl and, in mechanistic literature, lysyl, glutaminyl and tryptophanyl synthetases. These should be labelled emerging rather than treated as equivalent to the core eight-antibody clinical panel.

Deep clinical pearl: antisynthetase syndrome is not “anti-Jo-1 syndrome”. Jo-1 negativity plus normal CK does not exclude ASyS when unexplained NSIP/OP-pattern ILD, Raynaud, inflammatory arthritis or subtle mechanic’s hands point in that direction.

Signature: severe necrotizing myopathy and very high CK. Think next: IMNM first; lung attribution needs caution.

IMNM · MSA

Signature: immune-mediated necrotizing myopathy; statin-associated in some adults, but not all. Think next: persistent weakness/CK despite statin withdrawal.

IBM-ASSOCIATED

cN1A / NT5C1A

Signature: supportive marker for inclusion-body myositis, not disease-specific. Think next: finger-flexor/quadriceps pattern and dysphagia.

OVERLAP · MAA

Signature: myositis–systemic-sclerosis overlap, often with ILD/Raynaud/sclerodermatous features. Think next: overlap syndrome.

OVERLAP · MAA

Ku

Signature: overlap autoimmunity with myositis and possible ILD. Think next: search for SSc/SLE overlap features.

MODIFIER · MAA

Ro52 / TRIM21

Signature: context-dependent severity modifier, especially with MDA5 and several antisynthetase phenotypes. Think next: interaction, not standalone destiny.

OVERLAP CTD

U1-RNP · Scl-70 · fibrillarin · Th/To

Signature: connective-tissue overlap rather than classic MSA biology. Think next: the pulmonary phenotype may be driven by the overlap CTD context.

ANTISYNTHETASE SPECTRUM

Not just Jo-1.

HISTIDYL-tRNA SYNTHETASE

Jo-1

Most common ARS. Classic ASyS pattern: myositis, ILD, arthritis, Raynaud, fever and mechanic's hands.

THREONYL-tRNA SYNTHETASE

PL-7

Often lung-heavy; ILD may dominate or precede myositis. Brain cue: Jo-1-negative NSIP/OP does not end the ASyS search.

ALANYL-tRNA SYNTHETASE

PL-12

Frequently pulmonary-predominant and sometimes relatively amyopathic. Brain cue: subtle systemic clues can matter.

GLYCYL-tRNA SYNTHETASE

EJ

Important lung-predominant ARS with acute/subacute presentations described. Brain cue: do not wait for dramatic CK elevation.

ISOLEUCYL-tRNA SYNTHETASE

OJ

Rare and technically challenging on some routine panels. Brain cue: phenotype–assay discordance may justify confirmation.

ASPARAGINYL-tRNA SYNTHETASE

KS

Rare, often ILD-predominant with limited muscle manifestations. Brain cue: amyopathic does not mean non-antisynthetase.

PHENYLALANYL-tRNA SYNTHETASE

Zo

Very rare. Antibody-specific prognosis is hard to establish from small cohorts. Brain cue: avoid overclaiming from case-level data.

TYROSYL-tRNA SYNTHETASE

Ha / YRS

Rare ARS with limited cohort-level evidence. Brain cue: prioritize reproducible phenotype over novelty.

Atlas rule: weak bands, multiple simultaneous MSAs, unexpected phenotypes and rare specificities should trigger review of assay method and signal strength and, when clinically consequential, confirmatory testing.
